## Recommendations for the Safe Application of Temporal Interference Stimulation in the Human Brain (Bioelectromagnetics)

**Antonino M. Cassarà, Taylor H. Newton, Katie Zhuang, Sabine J. Regel, Peter Achermann, Alvaro Pascual-Leone, Niels Kuster, and Esra Neufeld**

**Part I: Principles of Electrical Neuromodulation and Adverse Effects, in the Special issue of Bioelectromagnetics on Neurostimulation 2025, Volume 46, Issue 2, online 07 February 2025; doi: 10.1002/bem.22542**

**Part II: Biophysics, Dosimetry, and Safety Recommendations, in the Special issue of Bioelectromagnetics on Neurostimulation 2025, Volume 46, Issue 1, online 15 January 2025; doi: 10.1002/bem.22536**

Temporal interference stimulation (TIS) is an emerging form of transcranial electrical stimulation that offers the unique ability to target deep brain structures non-invasively. While TIS holds promise for various clinical and research applications, limited data is available regarding its effects in humans and mechanisms of action. To inform the design and safe conduct of TIS experiments, researchers require quantitative guidance regarding exposure limits and other safety considerations.

In this two-part study, researchers at the IT'IS Foundation, TI Solutions AG, and collaborating institutions have established the first safety framework for TIS. Part I comprises a thorough review of current knowledge about TIS and related techniques, including the electrophysiological impacts of neurostimulation, mechanisms of action, and a systematic survey of adverse effects reported in scientific literature and regulatory databases. Building on this foundation, Part II employs advanced computational modeling to determine field exposure metrics for various stimulation scenarios in an anatomically detailed head model. By matching stimulation conditions across different modalities, the researchers derive frequency-dependent current thresholds below which TIS is unlikely to pose risks in terms of heating or unwanted stimulation.

The study provides crucial guidance to the scientific and clinical communities and represents a significant step toward enabling the safe translation of TIS technologies into clinical application. The proposed safety guidelines are based on comprehensive modeling of electric fields, current densities, charge injection, neural activation, and induced heating, ensuring that exposures remain consistent with current best practices for transcranial stimulation.

The scientific and technical impact of the study can be summarized as:

* A review of adverse effects and safety considerations from related neurostimulation techniques was conducted to inform TIS safety guidelines
* Advanced computational modeling was used to compare exposure conditions between TIS and established brain stimulation methods
* Frequency-dependent current thresholds are proposed to ensure TIS can be safely applied in research and clinical settings
* Practical guidance on electrode configurations and stimulation parameters is provided to minimize risks while maintaining therapeutic efficacy
