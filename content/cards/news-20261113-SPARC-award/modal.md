## SPARC - A Sustainable Repository for Datasets and Tools Bridging the Body and the Brain

We are pleased to announce that IT’IS USA will be part of the continued development and operation of the [SPARC Portal](https://osparc.io), with renewed [NIH funding](https://reporter.nih.gov/search/MHoHk6xl40mbOdQto-rIkQ/project-details/11264491). As part of this effort, IT’IS USA in collaboration with IT’IS will maintain and further expand the [o²S²PARC](https://sparc.science/tools-and-resources/4LkLiH5s4FV0LVJd3htsvH) simulation platform, building on the foundation established by the IT’IS Foundation SIM-Core Team in the past 8 years (see more details {{< modal-link news-20250904-oSPARC-Year8 >}}here{{< /modal-link >}}).

Originally created to support the NIH Stimulating Peripheral Activity to Relieve Conditions (SPARC) program launched in 2017, the SPARC Portal has evolved into an open resource for neuroscience and systems physiology, bringing together datasets, knowledge, computational models, and spatial mapping. In addition, the o²S²PARC platform matured into a unique cloud-based environment for collaborative model development, execution, visualization, and analysis. It enables predictive multiscale and multiphysics simulations, connecting peripheral nerve stimulation with organ-level responses.

The new 5-year award is led by Prof. Joost Wagenaar at the University of Pennsylvania and supported through NIH’s U24 mechanism for the enhancement and management of established biomedical data repositories and knowledgebases.

IT’IS USA will focus on ensuring that SPARC’s growing data resources can fully benefit from o²S²PARC’s extensive and continuously evolving modeling capabilities, such as tools for neural-interface safety and effectiveness assessments. It will also ensure the reliable operation of the platform and keep its underlying technologies up to date with advances in web technologies. Platform capabilities will be further expanded, with new functionalities such as text-to-code for computational modeling and model-predictive control. Both IT’IS organizations will also support users in publishing their computational models in line with FAIRl principles and contribute to the sustainable, long-term operation of the SPARC Portal and SPARC.

We look forward to working closely with an outstanding group of collaborators, helping sustain and strengthen o²S²PARC and the SPARC Portal as a cornerstone resource for the neuromodulation research community.

NIH grant PAR-23-237 award no. [1U24EB038234-01](https://reporter.nih.gov/search/MHoHk6xl40mbOdQto-rIkQ/project-details/11264491)
Project Period: April 15, 2026 – February 28, 2031
