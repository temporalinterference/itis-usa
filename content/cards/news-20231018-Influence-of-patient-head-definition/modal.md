## Influence of patient head definition on induced E-fields during MR examination (Magn. Reson. Med.)

**Tolga Goren, Sylvain Reboux, Silvia Farcito, Bryn Lloyd and Niels Kuster, Magnetic Resonance in Medicine, online 18 October 2023; doi: 10.1002/mrm.29894**

Radiofrequency (RF) exposure during magnetic resonance (MR) examination is limited by IEC 60601-2-33 to prevent thermal hazards to patients; these limits are used to derive the maximum induced field for the demonstration of MR safety of implants per ISO/TS 10974 (2018). One limit is the head-averaged specific absorption rate (SAR), for which the head extent is defined differently by MR and implant vendors.
The extents of the head used by major implant vendors and defined in ISO/TS 10974 (2018) are larger than those introduced in IEC 60601-2-33 (2022), resulting in lower estimates of the RF head exposure by up to 2.4 dB (factor 1.7) at Normal and First Level Operating Mode in the study of twelve [Virtual Population](https://itis.swiss/virtual-population/virtual-population/overview/ (ViP) phantoms. These anatomical phantoms were exposed to the RF fields of ten different sizes of whole-body volume transmit birdcage coils over imaging landmarks from the center of the head to the lower extremities, as part of the [MRIxViP1.5T](https://itis.swiss/virtual-population/explib/mrixvip1-5t/) and [MRIxViP3.0T](https://itis.swiss/virtual-population/explib/mrixvip3-0t/) exposure libraries qualified by the U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA).

This inconsistency may result in under- or over-estimation of the risks posed by the RF exposure in the head tissues during SAR-limited MR examinations. In other words, the definition used by the implant vendors, and potentially by others, results in lower maximum field strength and has the potential to under-estimate the risk when the MR vendors used a different definition to define their applied levels.
The existence of two incompatible definitions of the head in two related standards should be addressed by the community, and future revisions of MR standards should eliminate this inconsistency. Alternative definitions for implant safety assessments, namely, specification of maximum B1+ levels instead of SAR-limited operating modes, would make the assessment independent of the definition of head extents, and would also address the problem.

The impact of this technical note can be summarized as:

* Definitions of the head extent used by major implant vendors and defined in ISO/TS 10974 (2018) are larger than those introduced in IEC 60601-2-33 (2022) by MR system vendors
* The 2022 MR system vendor definition results in the potential for higher RF head exposure by up to 2.4 dB (factor 1.7) in the IT'IS [ViP](https://itis.swiss/virtual-population/virtual-population/overview/), compared to the 2018 definition of ISO 10974
* Harmonizing the two related standards is urgently needed. Adopting the larger extent would reduce the maximum exposure and make the standard consistent with implant testing. In the meantime, all parties should specify which definition of the head was applied when estimating the maximum patient RF exposure during SAR-limited head and shoulder MR examinations
* Where possible, safety assessments should be referenced to B1+ levels, if regulators and MR technicians are comfortable with such labeling
